# Relativos y deducción

Cerramos B1 con herramientas para escribir frases más largas y mejor conectadas: los **pronombres relativos** (el "que", "donde" y "cuyo" del inglés), los modales para **sacar conclusiones** (*must, might, can't*) y los **conectores** de contraste y resultado.

## Día 1 — Who, which, that, where y whose

| Relativo | Para | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| **who** | personas | *The woman **who** called you is my aunt.* |
| **which** | cosas y animales | *The bus **which** goes downtown is the 60.* |
| **that** | personas o cosas | *The book **that** I'm reading is great.* |
| **where** | lugares | *That's the school **where** I studied.* |
| **whose** | posesión (cuyo, de quien) | *I met a man **whose** son plays for Boca.* |

Dos reglas:

- Si el relativo es el **objeto**, se puede sacar: *The book (that) I'm reading.* Si es el **sujeto**, no: *The woman **who** called.*
- No repitas el objeto: *the movie that I saw* (no *the movie that I saw it*).

Y nunca uses **what** después de un sustantivo: *the phone **that** I bought* (no *the phone what I bought*).

## Día 2 — Especificativas y explicativas

| Especificativa (*defining*) | Explicativa (*non-defining*) |
|---|---|
| Dice **de quién** o **de qué** hablás. | Agrega un **dato extra**. |
| **Sin** comas. | **Entre** comas. |
| Acepta *that* y se puede omitir el relativo si es objeto. | **No** acepta *that* ni se puede omitir. |
| *The students **who passed** got a certificate.* | *My sister, **who lives in Spain**, is a nurse.* |

Las comas cambian el sentido:

- *My brother **who lives in Rosario** is an engineer.* → Tengo varios hermanos; hablo del que vive en Rosario.
- *My brother, **who lives in Rosario**, is an engineer.* → Tengo un solo hermano; el dato es extra.

Para "lo que" o "lo cual", referido a toda la oración anterior, se usa **which**: *He didn't call, **which** was strange.*

## Día 3 — Must, might y can't: deducción

| Seguridad | Modal | Ejemplo |
|---|---|---|
| Seguro que sí | **must** | *She's been working all day. She **must** be tired.* |
| Puede ser | **might / may / could** | *He **might** be in a meeting.* |
| Seguro que no | **can't** | *That **can't** be Juan; he's in Japan.* |

Para algo que está pasando ahora: **modal + be + -ing**: *They **must be having** lunch.*

Ojo: en una deducción, lo contrario de *must* es **can't**, no *mustn't* (*mustn't* es prohibición).

## Día 4 — Although, however y so

| Conector | Qué expresa | Cómo se usa |
|---|---|---|
| **although / even though** | contraste | + oración: ***Although** it was raining, we went out.* |
| **despite / in spite of** | contraste | + sustantivo o *-ing*: ***Despite** the rain, we went out.* |
| **however** | contraste | al principio de otra oración, con coma: *It was cheap. **However**, it was dirty.* |
| **so** | resultado | *I was tired, **so** I went to bed.* |
| **because** | causa | *I went to bed **because** I was tired.* |

No mezcles *although* con *but*: ***Although** I studied, I failed* (sin *but*).

## Día 5 — Escribir una reseña

Una reseña presenta el lugar, cuenta lo bueno y lo malo y termina con una recomendación.

> ***A hidden gem in Colonia***
>
> *Last weekend we stayed at Casa Verde, a small hotel **which** is right next to the river. The owner, **who** is originally from Montevideo, was really friendly. Our room had a balcony **where** we had breakfast every morning. The view at sunset **must** be the best in town! **Although** the beds were a bit small, we slept well. The restaurant was slow. **However**, the food was delicious, so we didn't mind waiting. I'd definitely recommend it to anyone **who** wants a quiet weekend.*

Frases útiles: *I'd definitely recommend it. / It's worth a visit. / The only downside was...*

## En pocas palabras

- **who** (personas), **which** (cosas), **that** (los dos), **where** (lugares), **whose** (posesión).
- Con comas, dato extra y nunca *that*; sin comas, información necesaria.
- **must** = seguro que sí, **might** = puede ser, **can't** = seguro que no.
